# Multi-factor authentication

::: tip Who can do this
Anyone, for themselves. Which methods are on offer is your organisation's
choice.
:::

Multi-factor authentication (MFA) adds a second verification step on top of your
password. Four methods are supported, and you can enrol in more than one — the
more methods you have, the easier it is to recover if you lose access to a
device.

You manage MFA from the **Multi-Factor Authentication** section of your
[Account page](/account/managing-your-account) in Console.

## Available methods

| Method | What it is | When to use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Authenticator App (TOTP)** | A 6-digit code refreshed every 30 seconds, generated by an app such as Google Authenticator, Microsoft Authenticator or 1Password. | Best balance of security and convenience. Works offline. The recommended default. |
| **Email OTP** | A one-time code emailed to you. | A fallback when you do not have your phone. |
| **SMS OTP** | A one-time code sent to your verified phone number. | Only if your organisation enables it; less secure than TOTP. |
| **Passkey / Security Key** | A WebAuthn credential bound to a device or hardware key. | The strongest option. Phishing-resistant. |

Your organisation chooses which of these are allowed. If a method is not listed
on your Account page, your customer's admin has not enabled it.

## Enrolling an authenticator app

1. Click **Set up Authenticator App**.
2. A dialog shows a QR code and a manual secret string.
3. In your authenticator app, scan the QR code or paste the secret.
4. The app starts generating 6-digit codes.
5. Enter the current code in the verification field and click **Verify**.

The method is now active and appears in your enabled list.

## Enabling email OTP

1. Click **Enable Email OTP**.
2. A test code is sent to your verified email.
3. Enter the code to confirm you received it.

Email OTP is now enabled.

## Adding a passkey

1. Click **Add Passkey**.
2. You are sent an email with a setup link.
3. Open the link on the device, or with the hardware key, you want to enrol.
4. Follow the system prompt to create the passkey.

The passkey is registered against your account.

::: info Passkeys live on the device
Passkeys are managed by your operating system or browser, not by Console. If
you lose the device, remove its passkey here and enrol a new one.
:::

## Removing a method

Click **Remove** next to the method. A confirmation dialog warns you about
losing access.

::: warning Add a replacement first
If this is your only method and your organisation enforces MFA, removal is
blocked. Enrol another method, then remove this one.
:::

## If you lose access

If you cannot sign in because every one of your MFA devices is lost:

1. Contact your **customer admin** if you are a customer user, or an **Exto
   operator** if you are an internal user.
2. They reset your MFA from your user detail page.
3. On your next sign-in you are prompted to enrol again.

There is no self-service recovery, and that is intentional: recovering an
account needs a human in the loop.

## Required or optional

Customer admins decide whether MFA is **required** or **optional** for users in
their organisation, under **Settings → Security** in the Customer Portal. If it
is required and you have not enrolled yet, you are guided through enrolment the
first time you sign in.
